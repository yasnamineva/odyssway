You miscounted. Your 90 days in Europe ran out last week, or your flight was
cancelled and you're still here. What happens now?

Probably less than you fear, and possibly more than you hope. The rules are
set by EU law, but each country applies them its own way.

## What EU law says

Every Schengen country applies the EU's Return Directive. Its basic process:

1. **A return decision.** Once you're found staying without the right to,
   the country must formally tell you to leave.
2. **7 to 30 days to go.** The decision normally gives you a window to leave
   on your own. It can be longer, for example if you have children in school.
3. **A ban only in some cases.** An entry ban is required if you weren't given
   a window, or didn't leave within it. Otherwise it's up to the country.
   Bans are normally up to **five years**.

So there's no automatic ban for an overstay. Leaving within the window you're
given is what counts most.

## Country rules differ

The fines and bans are set nationally:

- **France** doesn't fine a simple overstay. The risk starts if you're
  ordered to leave and don't: ignoring a removal order can mean up to three
  years in prison and a ten-year ban from France.
- **Spain** chooses either a fine of €501 to €10,000 **or** expulsion with a
  ban of three to ten years. Spanish courts favour a fine for a simple
  overstay.
- **The Netherlands** usually bans you for a year if you overstayed by more
  than 3 and up to 90 days, and two years beyond that. The ban starts the day you leave.
- **Germany**: entry bans are up to five years as the general rule, and much
  longer only after a criminal conviction or for security reasons.

## You'll be noticed now

Before 2026, a short overstay might go unseen. Today the EU's Entry/Exit
System logs every crossing and [flags overstays automatically](/blog/europe-counts-your-days).

## If you're stuck or wrongly flagged

- **Contact the immigration authority before your stay runs out** if
  something outside your control, like illness or a cancelled flight, is
  keeping you. The EES Regulation lets overstay records be corrected for
  unforeseeable, serious events.
- **Wrongly flagged?** If you did leave on time, you can have the record
  [corrected](/ees/dispute-overstay).
- **Plan ahead.** Check your dates with our [calculator](/calculator) before
  booking a return flight.

This is general information, not legal advice. If you've received a return
decision, talk to the authority that issued it or a qualified adviser.
