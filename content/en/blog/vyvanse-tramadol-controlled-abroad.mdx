Adderall's active ingredient, amphetamine, is on the UN's international list of
controlled psychotropic substances. So is methylphenidate, in Ritalin. Every
country that signed the UN conventions has to control them, so their rules
abroad follow a recognisable pattern.

**Vyvanse (lisdexamfetamine) and tramadol are different.** Neither is on the
UN's international lists, which are kept by the International Narcotics Control
Board (INCB). Each country decides for itself, and the answers range from
"not controlled at all" to "only with a permit applied for in advance".

## Vyvanse, country by country

- **United States:** a Schedule II controlled substance. You can bring it for
  your own use in its original container, and declare it.
- **United Kingdom:** a Schedule 2 drug. You can bring up to 3 months' supply
  in your hand luggage; if you don't live in the UK, carry a letter from your
  prescriber.
- **Canada:** Canada's schedule covers amphetamines and their derivatives, and
  we read that as including Vyvanse. Carry it yourself: up to 30 days' supply
  or one course of treatment, in pharmacy packaging, and declare it.
- **Ireland:** a Schedule 2 drug. Arriving from another Schengen country, you
  need an Article 75 certificate; from elsewhere, up to 3 months' supply with a
  prescription or doctor's letter.
- **Japan:** a "stimulants' raw material". You need permission from Japan's
  Narcotics Control Department before you travel. (Adderall itself can't be
  brought into Japan at all.)
- **Singapore:** on HSA's list of controlled substances, so you need HSA
  approval, applied for at least 2 weeks before you arrive.
- **United Arab Emirates:** on the UAE's controlled-medicines schedule. You can
  bring up to 3 months' supply with a recent stamped prescription *and* an
  attested medical report.

## Tramadol, country by country

- **United States:** Schedule IV, under the same personal-use rule as above.
- **United Kingdom:** Schedule 3, also covered by the 3-month rule.
- **Canada:** Schedule I, so the 30-day limit applies.
- **Japan:** Japan's Narcotics Control Department says tramadol isn't a
  narcotic there, so the narcotics import certificate doesn't apply to it.
- **Singapore:** tramadol isn't on HSA's controlled-substances list, so the
  ordinary personal-medication rules appear to apply. Check the current list
  before you travel.
- **Qatar:** on Qatar's list of psychotropic substances, so you need a
  Ministry of Public Health permit.

## Travelling in Europe

The Schengen rule (Article 75) lets you carry "narcotic drugs and psychotropic
substances" with a certificate from your home country. Because Vyvanse and
tramadol aren't on the UN lists, whether you need that certificate for them
depends on each country's own law. Ireland, for example, lists lisdexamfetamine
in its Schedule 2.
