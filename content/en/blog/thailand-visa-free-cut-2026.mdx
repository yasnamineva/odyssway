On 15 September 2026, Thailand quietly took away half of most visitors'
visa-free time. If you hold a US, UK, Canadian, Australian, Japanese or EU
passport, you used to get 60 days on arrival. Now you get 30.

It's the biggest change in our data this year, but not the only one. Here
are five rules that moved in 2026, or are about to.

## 1. Thailand: 60 days became 30

Thailand replaced its 60-day visa exemption with a 30-day one for tourism.
You can ask the Immigration Bureau for one extension of up to 30 days once
you're there. If you entered before 15 September under the old scheme, you
keep the stay you were given.

Two things to watch:

- **A few passports kept 90 days.** South Korea, Brazil, Argentina and Chile
  have their own bilateral agreements with Thailand, so their citizens still
  get up to 90 days.
- **Mexico lost visa-free entry entirely.** Since 15 September, Mexican
  citizens need a visa for any trip to Thailand, applied for through the
  e-Visa system.

There is also a limit on how often you can use the exemption: twice per
calendar year. Thailand's embassy in London states it generally, while the
Tourism Authority describes it as a limit on land-border entries. If you
plan to hop in and out of Thailand, check before you go.

## 2. South Korea: the free pass ends on 31 December

Most visitors to Korea are supposed to get a K-ETA, an online travel
authorisation, before flying. Since April 2023, Korea has waived that
for 22 countries, including the US, UK, Australia, Japan, Germany and France. The
waiver has been extended several times. It currently runs until
**31 December 2026**.

If it isn't extended again, travellers from those countries will need a
K-ETA from 1 January. It costs 10,000 won and lasts three years. Not everyone
was on the waiver list: Mexican, Argentine, Chilean and Israeli citizens
already need one.

Canadians get a better deal than most: up to 180 days visa-free, not 90.

## 3. China: visa-free for many, with an end date

China lets citizens of the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South
Korea, Brazil, Argentina, Chile and several EU countries visit for up to
30 days without a visa. One detail catches people out: the 30 days are
counted from midnight the day *after* you arrive.

For the UK, Canada and the European countries we cover, China says the policy
runs until **31 December 2026**. US citizens are not on the list and still
need a tourist visa.

## 4. Brazil: visas are back for three countries

Since 10 April 2025, US, Canadian and Australian citizens need a visa for
Brazil again. Brazil's official visa
table from August 2026 confirms it's still in force. Canada's government puts the e-visa at about US$80.90, valid
five years.

UK and most European visitors are unaffected: up to 90 days, no visa.

## 5. Vietnam: 45 days, guaranteed until 2028

Vietnam went the other way. Citizens of the UK, Japan, South Korea,
Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands can stay up to 45 days
without a visa, and the policy runs until 14 March 2028. US, Canadian and
Australian citizens still need an e-visa, which covers up to 90 days.

## Before you book

Rules like these change with a cabinet decision, and old blog posts keep
repeating the previous number for years. Check your exact passport and
destination in [Trip Check](/trip-check). Every answer there links to the
government page it comes from, so you can see the date it was checked.
