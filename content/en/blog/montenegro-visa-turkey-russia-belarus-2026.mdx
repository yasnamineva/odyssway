On 1 November 2026, Montenegro stopped letting Turkish, Russian and
Belarusian citizens in without a visa. The Government decided this on
23 July 2026 to bring Montenegro's visa rules in line with the European
Union's. Chinese citizens are affected too: the two narrow exemptions they
had (organised tour groups, and business-passport holders with an
invitation) ended on 31 October.

If you hold one of these passports and have a trip to Montenegro planned,
here's what changed and what you can do.

## What you need now

You need a Montenegrin short-stay (C) visa before you travel. Montenegro's
Foreign Ministry says applications can be made at VFS Global centres,
including in Türkiye (Ankara, Istanbul) and Russia (Moscow and several
other cities), as well as at Montenegrin embassies. A C visa allows at most
90 days in any 180-day period.

When you apply, you'll need travel health insurance, and your passport must
have been issued within the last 10 years, be valid at least 3 months after
you plan to leave Montenegro, and have two blank pages.

## The exception: you already hold certain visas

You can still enter Montenegro **without a Montenegrin visa for up to 30
days** if you hold a valid:

- Schengen visa, or a residence permit from a Schengen country,
- US visa or residence permit,
- UK visa or residence permit, or
- Irish visa or residence permit.

If that visa expires sooner than 30 days after you arrive, you can only
stay until it expires.

## Once you're there

Whatever passport you hold, you must register your stay with the police
within 24 hours of arriving at each place you stay. Hotels usually do it
for you; in private accommodation you can register through the local
tourist organisation.
