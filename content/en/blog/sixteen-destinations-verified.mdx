We just added 13 destinations to Trip Check — Egypt, Morocco, Nigeria,
Rwanda, India, the UAE, Thailand, Turkey, and Australia among them — bringing
the total to 16, on top of the Schengen Area. Here's the part that actually
matters: how, not just how many.

## The rule we don't break, even to move faster

Every entry requirement and every customs verdict on this site has to trace
to a primary official source — that destination's own immigration authority,
its own customs authority, the actual regulation — checked directly, cited,
and dated. Not a travel blog's summary of the rule. Not what we remember
being true. Not a plausible guess filled in to complete a table.

That constraint is slower than the alternative. It's also the entire reason
this site is worth trusting over one that answers instantly for every
country by inference.

## What "verified" caught, repeatedly

Doing this properly for 13 countries at once surfaced real problems a faster
process would have missed:

- **Nigeria discontinued visa-on-arrival in May 2025.** Several sites still
  describe the old system. We found the current one.
- **Thailand's 60-day visa-exemption is cabinet-approved for a cut to 30 or
  15 days — but not yet published in the Royal Gazette.** We published the
  rule that's actually in force today, not the one that's coming.
- **A government-branded e-Visa domain for one country now redirects to a
  private company.** The security certificate is genuine, so it's likely a
  legitimate contractor handover — but we didn't cite anything from that
  domain until we understand what happened, and we're flagging it for a
  closer look.
- **Two independent reads of the same official page gave contradictory
  answers** on which nationalities need a visa — the single highest-stakes
  question that page answers. We caught it by re-parsing the raw page
  instead of trusting a summarized read, and we're carrying that lesson
  into every source we check from here on.

## What we didn't publish

Just as important: several facts we went looking for — specific medication
quantity limits, drone customs rules, a handful of stay-length figures — 
simply aren't published anywhere we could find, for any of these countries.
We left those gaps open rather than fill them with a plausible-sounding
number. You'll see "not yet verified" in those spots, and that's
deliberate, not an oversight.

## Where this goes next

Brazil is next in the queue. After that, we're prioritizing by two things:
how much real travel demand there is, and what we learned about which
countries' government sites are actually reachable and current — some
research passes go smoothly, some involve real detective work, and we'd
rather tell you that honestly than pretend every country is equally easy to
verify.
