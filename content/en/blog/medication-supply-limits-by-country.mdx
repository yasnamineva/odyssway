Most countries let you bring your own medicine for personal use. How much you can bring
varies a lot, though: from 15 days' supply to 6 months, and many countries don't name a
number at all. Here is what the official rules say in the countries we've checked.

The first four sections cover **ordinary prescription medicine**. Strong painkillers,
ADHD stimulants and many sleeping pills are controlled medicines, and they often have
tighter rules. Those come at the end.

## Up to 3 months (90 days)

The most common limit:

- [United States](/bring/medication/united-states): about a 90-day supply, in the
  original containers, with your prescription or a doctor's note.
- [United Kingdom](/bring/medication/united-kingdom): up to 3 months' supply without a
  licence.
- [Canada](/bring/medication/canada): a 90-day supply or one course of treatment,
  whichever is less, in labelled packaging.
- [Ireland](/bring/medication/ireland): up to 3 months' supply if you arrive from
  outside the Schengen Area.
- [Australia](/bring/medication/australia): up to a 3-month supply under the
  traveller's exemption.
- [Singapore](/bring/medication/singapore): up to 3 months' supply prescribed to you,
  with a prescription copy and pharmacy-labelled packaging.
- [South Korea](/bring/medication/south-korea): at most 6 bottles or three months'
  supply.
- [South Africa](/bring/medication/south-africa): up to three months' use, with a
  prescription from a doctor.
- [Rwanda](/bring/medication/rwanda): up to a 90-day supply, with a prescription or a
  letter from an authorised practitioner.
- [Maldives](/bring/medication/maldives): up to 90 days' supply, with a prescription
  less than 3 months old.
- [Sri Lanka](/bring/medication/sri-lanka): up to 90 days' supply, or the length of your
  stay if that's shorter.
- [Israel](/bring/medication/israel): up to a 90-day supply at the recommended dosage.

## Shorter limits

- [Serbia](/bring/medication/serbia): enough for 15 days of regular therapy without any
  paperwork. For more, up to six months, you need approval from the Ministry of Health
  first.
- [Tanzania](/bring/medication/tanzania): enough for up to 21 days of treatment. More
  needs a TMDA permit.
- [Japan](/bring/medication/japan): up to a 1-month supply of prescription drugs (2
  months for ordinary over-the-counter medicine). More needs a *Yakkan Shoumei*
  certificate, obtained in advance.
- [Thailand](/bring/medication/thailand): up to a 30-day supply.
- [Malaysia](/bring/medication/malaysia): no more than you'd reasonably need for one
  month, judged from the dosage on the label.
- [Albania](/bring/medication/albania): up to 2 months' supply, with a prescription and
  a signed personal-use statement left with customs.
- [Taiwan](/bring/medication/taiwan): up to 2 months' supply if you have no doctor's
  note.

## Longer limits

- [Montenegro](/bring/medication/montenegro): a reasonable amount, up to 6 months'
  supply.
- [Taiwan](/bring/medication/taiwan): up to 6 months' supply *with* a doctor's note or a
  copy of your prescription.

## No fixed number

These countries set no number of days. The test is a reasonable amount for your own use:

- [China](/bring/medication/china): small, reasonable quantities for your own use,
  subject to customs checks.
- [Hong Kong](/bring/medication/hong-kong): a reasonable quantity for personal use,
  carried yourself.
- [Brazil](/bring/medication/brazil): quantities that fit your trip. A prescription may
  be requested at the border.
- [Mexico](/bring/medication/mexico): medicines for personal use are allowed in your
  baggage.
- [Dominican Republic](/bring/medication/dominican-republic): quantities consistent with
  the length of your stay and your dosage.
- [Costa Rica](/bring/medication/costa-rica): quantities consistent with your
  circumstances and needs.
- [Chile](/bring/medication/chile): quantities consistent with your prescription. The
  health institute (ISP) refers to up to 6 months of treatment.
- [Peru](/bring/medication/peru): medicines for your own use count as personal effects
  and don't need to be declared.
- [Turkey](/bring/medication/turkey): medicine for use during your trip, with documents
  showing you need it.

## Controlled medicines: when you need a permit before you travel

In these countries, you need a permit for controlled medicine before you arrive. Some
take two weeks or more to process:

- [Japan](/bring/medication/japan): narcotics such as oxycodone and codeine, and
  stimulant raw materials such as Vyvanse, need a certificate from the Narcotics Control
  Department. Apply at least 14 days ahead. Adderall can't be brought in at all.
- [Singapore](/bring/medication/singapore): medicines on HSA's controlled list need HSA
  approval, applied for at least 2 weeks before you arrive.
- [Hong Kong](/bring/medication/hong-kong): medicine containing dangerous drugs needs
  prior written approval from the Drug Office. Processing normally takes 10 working
  days.
- [South Korea](/bring/medication/south-korea): a permit from the Ministry of Food and
  Drug Safety before arrival.
- [Saudi Arabia](/bring/medication/saudi-arabia): clearance through SFDA's online
  Controlled Drugs System before you travel.
- [Qatar](/bring/medication/qatar): a Controlled Medication Permit from the Ministry of
  Public Health.
- [Cyprus](/bring/medication/cyprus): a permit from the Pharmaceutical Services.
- [Jamaica](/bring/medication/jamaica): a special permit from the Ministry of Health and
  Wellness for prescriptions containing narcotics.
- [Serbia](/bring/medication/serbia): Ministry of Health approval, however short your
  trip.
- [India](/bring/medication/india): India's Department of Revenue says travellers can
  ask the Narcotics Commissioner for permission well before they leave.

In some countries you only need a permit above a certain amount:

- [Thailand](/bring/medication/thailand): psychotropic medicines up to 30 days need only
  your doctor's certificate or prescription. For 31 to 90 days you need a Thai FDA
  permit, at least 15 days before you travel. Narcotic medicines always need that
  permit.
- [Philippines](/bring/medication/philippines): up to 30 days with a doctor's letter and
  prescription. More needs PDEA authorization, requested 30 working days before you
  arrive.
- [Costa Rica](/bring/medication/costa-rica): up to one month of treatment without
  authorisation. More needs Ministry of Health authorisation, applied for at least one
  month before you travel.
- [Tanzania](/bring/medication/tanzania): at most 30 days of treatment with an English
  prescription. Larger quantities need a TMDA permit.

And in the [United Arab Emirates](/bring/medication/united-arab-emirates), a permit is
optional. You can bring up to 3 months' supply with a recent stamped prescription *and*
an attested medical report.
