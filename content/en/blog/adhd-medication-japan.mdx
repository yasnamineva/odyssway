Japan is strict about medicine, stricter than most travellers expect. If you take Adderall, the rule is short:
**you cannot bring it into Japan.** Not with a prescription, not with a
doctor's letter, not for a short trip.

## Why Adderall is banned

Adderall's active ingredient is amphetamine. Under Japan's Stimulants
Control Act, amphetamine and methamphetamine are banned outright, even for
medical treatment and even if your own country prescribed them. There's no
permit to apply for.

So the conversation to have is with your doctor, well before you fly.

## Vyvanse: allowed, with paperwork

Vyvanse (lisdexamfetamine) is classed differently: it's a "stimulants'
raw material". You can bring it, but only with advance permission from
the Narcotics Control Department of Japan's health ministry.

- Apply **at least 14 days** before you travel.
- Applications usually need a certificate from your doctor.
- Carry the approval with you.

## The cold medicine trap

The same rule covers pseudoephedrine, the decongestant in Sudafed and
many other cold remedies, and Vicks inhalers. They need the same advance
permission as Vyvanse. It's an easy one to pack without thinking.

## Your other medicine

Ordinary medicine is far simpler. Japan Customs lets you bring:

- up to **one month's supply** of prescription medicine,
- up to **two months' supply** of over-the-counter medicine,
- up to **24 pieces** of each external product, like creams.

Bringing more needs an import certificate called a Yakkan Shoumei, arranged
in advance. Diabetes and weight-loss injections such as Ozempic count as
prescription medicine, so the one-month limit applies (more in
[our Ozempic guide](/blog/ozempic-wegovy-mounjaro-travel)).
