CBD is easy to buy in many countries, so it rarely feels like something to
check before a trip. At the border it's a different story: in
some places a CBD oil or gummy is treated exactly like cannabis, even with
no THC in it. We've checked the rules against official sources for the
countries below.

**Short version:** don't bring any CBD product to Hong Kong, Singapore,
South Korea, the UAE or Indonesia. New Zealand, Australia, Taiwan and Brazil
allow it only with a prescription or a permit arranged before you travel.
For a country not listed here, assume nothing and check first.

## Banned, even with no THC

**Hong Kong.** Since 1 February 2023, CBD has been a dangerous drug in Hong
Kong, in any concentration. That covers oils, skincare, food and drinks.
Importing it can mean life imprisonment and a HK$5 million fine.

**Singapore.** Cannabis, cannabis extracts, THC and CBD are absolutely
prohibited, regardless of prescription or approval. The usual
controlled-medicine approval process doesn't apply.

**South Korea.** Korea Customs lists CBD with THC as a cannabis ingredient
under the Narcotics Control Act. Bringing it in is punishable by life
imprisonment or at least 5 years, even for medicines sold or prescribed
abroad. The one
route is prior approval from the Minister of Food and Drug Safety for
medicine for your own treatment.

**United Arab Emirates.** According to the UK government's travel advice,
CBD oil is illegal in the UAE. Skincare products and e-cigarette refills
containing it are confiscated, and you could face criminal charges.

**Indonesia, Armenia and Tanzania.** The UK government's travel advice says
CBD products are illegal in Indonesia and Armenia, and that some are in
Tanzania. For Indonesia it names CBD, hemp-based products and medicinal
cannabis, in any form.

**Canada.** Taking cannabis across the border in any form, including oils
containing CBD, is a criminal offence without a Health Canada permit or
exemption, even with a medical document and even from a place where
cannabis is legal.

**Thailand, Cyprus and Vietnam.** Their rules ban cannabis extracts,
derivatives or preparations without naming CBD. Our reading is that CBD
products made from cannabis or hemp fall under these bans.

## Only with a prescription or a permit

**New Zealand.** You can bring a medicinal cannabis product prescribed to
you: up to **3 months' supply of a CBD product**, with a copy of the
prescription or a doctor's letter, in its original container, declared on
your New Zealand Traveller Declaration. Products supplied in the United
States aren't allowed, and without a prescription any CBD product counts as
a prescription medicine you can't import.

**Australia.** The traveller's exemption lets you bring medicinal cannabis
prescribed to you and supplied through a pharmacy, up to 3 months' supply.
Non-prescribed cannabis is prohibited.

**Taiwan.** CBD is an ordinary drug there, and none is approved for sale.
For a CBD medicine prescribed to you, apply to the Taiwan FDA for a special
import permit before you travel. Above 10 ppm THC, a product counts as a
narcotic.

**Brazil.** CBD products need ANVISA's exceptional import authorisation,
obtained before you travel. THC products are banned.

**Japan.** Products made only from the stalks or seeds of the plant need a
manufacturer's certificate and a lab analysis report saying so. Anything
made from other parts of the plant counts as cannabis and needs the health
ministry's permission.

**Chile.** CBD products are treated as medicines, and anything above 0.2%
THC is a narcotic. No cannabis cosmetics are authorised.

**China.** Since 1 September 2024, CBD has been a controlled precursor
chemical. The rules don't mention any allowance for travellers.

## Where the line is THC

**United States.** Under federal law, hemp CBD with no more than 0.3%
delta-9 THC isn't marijuana. That changes in two steps: from 12 November
2026 for cannabinoids a cannabis plant can't produce naturally, and from
11 December 2026 for the rest, including a cap of 0.4 mg total THC per
container. US Customs also warns that CBD products can contain more THC
than their labels say.

**Ireland.** CBD isn't a controlled drug, but THC in food is, at any level
(hemp seed products aside), and CBD food products are unauthorised novel
foods.

## Before you pack

- Check every country on your route, including where you change planes.
- Labels aren't a defence: a product sold as THC-free can still contain THC.
- If a country isn't listed here, we haven't verified it yet. Our country
  pages say so and link the official authority to ask.

Each country's page under [Can I bring cannabis or CBD?](/bring/cannabis) has
the full rule, its official source and the date we checked it.
