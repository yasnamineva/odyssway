Customs is the easy part of bringing a drone to Europe. There's no special customs
rule: the drone simply counts toward your personal-goods allowance (€300 if you
arrive by land, €430 by air or sea). Flying it is the part with rules, and it isn't
only EU law. Several countries add their own requirements on top.

Here's what the EU rules ask of a visitor, and what seven countries we've checked
add.

## The EU rules, for everyone

The EU Drone Regulation applies whatever your nationality.

- **Register once, in the first EU country you fly in.** If you live outside the EU,
  you register as a drone operator with that country's aviation authority. You get an
  operator number, which goes on a sticker on every drone and into its remote
  identification system. The registration then works across Europe.
- **When you don't need to register:** a drone under 250 g with no camera or other
  sensor that can capture personal data, or a drone under 250 g certified as a toy.
- **Training from home doesn't count.** You need to complete the EU training before
  you fly.
- **In the "open" category:** keep the drone in sight, fly no higher than 120 metres,
  carry no dangerous goods and drop nothing, and don't fly over people unless the
  drone has a class label or weighs under 250 g.
- **Check the map first.** Each country sets geographical zones where drones are
  restricted.

## What seven countries add

**France.** A drone of **800 g or more** must be registered by its owner on the
AlphaTango portal and carry an electronic signalling device. This is a national rule,
separate from EU operator registration, and it applies to operators from other EU
countries too. In the open category you can't fly in public space in built-up areas.
The minimum age is 14. Flying over a no-fly zone by negligence can bring six months
in prison and a €15,000 fine.
[France →](/bring/drones/france)

**Portugal.** Liability insurance is **compulsory for drones over 900 g**, with a
minimum cover that rises with the drone's weight. If Portugal is the first EU country
you fly in, you can register with the aviation authority (ANAC), but you need an
active Chave Móvel Digital, Portugal's digital ID, before you start.
[Portugal →](/bring/drones/portugal)

**Austria.** Drone insurance is compulsory. Registering with Austro Control costs
**€46.80**, lasts three years, needs your insurance policy number and is only for
people aged 18 or over. Austro Control says this applies even to short visits from
outside the EU, and that DJI Mini drones need registration too.
[Austria →](/bring/drones/austria)

**Germany.** If Germany is your first EU country, registration with the Federal
Aviation Office (LBA) costs **€20** and asks for an ID scan and your insurer's name
and policy number, so arrange drone liability insurance first.
[Germany →](/bring/drones/germany)

**Italy.** The aviation authority ENAC requires third-party liability insurance. If
you register in Italy, it's on the d-flight portal, and the QR code it gives you goes
on every drone you fly.
[Italy →](/bring/drones/italy)

**Spain.** Operators must be at least 16. Insurance is required for subcategory A2,
for A3 with a drone of 20 kg or more, and for the specific category. Check the zones
in the ENAIRE Drones app. Fines start at **€4,500** for minor infringements.
[Spain →](/bring/drones/spain)

**Netherlands.** You register with the vehicle authority (RDW), and the operator
number is valid for **one year**. Anyone you lend your drone to needs their own
number. No-fly zones are on the Aeret map.
[Netherlands →](/bring/drones/netherlands)

## Before you pack

- Register in the right country: the first EU country where you'll fly.
- Check insurance. Several of these countries require it, and in Germany and Austria
  you need the policy number to register.
- Look up the no-fly map for each country you'll fly in, not only the first.
- For a country not listed here, the EU rules are what we've verified so far. Our
  country pages say when that's the case.

Each country's page under [Can I bring a drone?](/bring/drones) has the full rule,
its official source and the date we checked it.
