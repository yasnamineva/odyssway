We just verified four more destinations — the Philippines, New Zealand,
Vietnam, and China — bringing Trip Check to 20 destinations covered, on top
of the Schengen Area. Two of them turned out to share something worth a
whole post: visa-free rules that look permanent but aren't.

## China's visa-free window for UK and Canadian travelers has an end date

In February 2026, China added the United Kingdom and Canada to its
unilateral 30-day visa-free list. Ordinary passport holders from either
country can now enter for business, tourism, family visits, cultural
exchange, or transit without a visa, with multiple entries permitted. US
nationals aren't on that list — they still apply in person through the
China Online Visa Application (COVA) system, submit biometrics, and show
proof of US residence.

Here's the part that's easy to miss: per China's National Immigration
Administration, the visa-free policy "currently runs through 31 December
2026." That's not a rolling deadline — it's a fixed one, a few months out
as we write this. Unless it's renewed, the rule simply lapses at the end
of the year. We'll be checking back before then.

## Vietnam's visa exemption for UK travelers has the same shape

UK and Northern Ireland citizens get 45 days visa-free in Vietnam under
Government Resolution No. 44/NQ-CP (7 March 2025) — but the resolution
itself is only in effect from 15 March 2025 to 14 March 2028. After that
date, unless it's extended, UK travelers go back to needing a visa or
e-visa, same as US and Canadian citizens already do today. (One thing that
*isn't* time-limited: the old 30-day re-entry waiting period was removed
under the 2019 Law on Entry, Exit, Transit and Residence of Foreigners,
effective 1 July 2020 — that part of the rule is settled.)

## New Zealand doesn't give every visa-waiver nationality the same stay

US and Canadian travelers get 90 days on the standard visa waiver. UK
citizens — and other British passport holders with the right to reside
permanently in the UK — get up to 6 months, a specific carve-out in
Immigration New Zealand's own rules. Everyone still needs the NZeTA
(NZ$17 via the app, NZ$23 via the website) plus the IVL levy (NZ$100),
valid for 2 years regardless of how long any single stay is.

## The Philippines added a step that has nothing to do with your visa

Executive Order No. 408 still governs the 30-day visa-free stay (the
first extension adds 29 more days, for a fee starting around PHP 3,000
depending on the immigration office). What's new is a separate,
mandatory requirement layered on top: every traveler now has to complete
eTravel registration (etravel.gov.ph) before arrival. It's easy to check
the old visa-free rule, see nothing's changed there, and miss that a new
step was added alongside it.
