Kratom, phenibut and poppers are easy to buy in some countries, in shops or
online. That tells you nothing about what happens when you land somewhere
else. For several popular destinations, the answer
is simple: **don't pack them.**

## Kratom

**Australia: not allowed.** Australia's customs rules list kratom (Mitragyna
speciosa) as a drug you can't import without a licence and a permit from the
health department. The only exception for travellers is a drug a doctor
prescribed to you, and Australia's Poisons Standard puts kratom in Schedule 9,
its list of prohibited substances.

**Japan: not allowed.** Kratom, and its main compounds mitragynine and
7-hydroxymitragynine, have been "designated substances" in Japan since 2016.
Japan Customs lists designated substances among the goods that can't be
imported, and having or using them is generally prohibited too.

**Singapore: not allowed, with severe penalties.** Mitragynine and
7-hydroxymitragynine are Class A controlled drugs in Singapore. Importing
kratom carries **at least 5 years in prison and 5 strokes of the cane**.
Possessing it can mean up to 10 years in prison.

**Sweden: not allowed.** Sweden classified kratom as a narcotic from 11 March
2025, and the government says Swedish Customs can now stop it at the border.
This is a Swedish rule, so it applies even if you're arriving from an EU
country where kratom is legal.

**New Zealand: only with a doctor's authority.** Kratom is a prescription
medicine in New Zealand. Medsafe says kratom found at the border is referred
to it and released only on the authority of a medical practitioner, who takes
responsibility for it. Kratom isn't approved for any medical use there.

## Phenibut

**Australia: not allowed.** Like kratom, phenibut is on Australia's customs
list of drugs that need an import licence and permit, and it's in Schedule 9
of the Poisons Standard.

## Poppers

**Japan: not allowed.** The nitrites in poppers, such as isobutyl,
isopropyl, isopentyl (isoamyl) and butyl nitrite, have been designated
substances in Japan since 2007. That puts them on Japan Customs' list of
prohibited imports. Amyl nitrite isn't on the list by that name, and we
haven't confirmed how Japan treats it, so check the label.

## What we haven't checked yet

We haven't verified the rules for kratom in the UK, Thailand or Malaysia, or
for poppers in Australia. If you're travelling there, check with the
country's customs or health authority. We'd rather say we don't know than
guess.
