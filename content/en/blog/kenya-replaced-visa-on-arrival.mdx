If the last thing you read about visiting Kenya mentioned "visa on arrival,"
it's out of date. Kenya replaced that system with a near-universal Electronic
Travel Authorisation (eTA), and the two work differently enough that
assuming the old rule still applies is a real way to get stuck at check-in.

## The actual change

Kenya's Citizenship and Immigration (Amendment) Regulations, 2023 — Legal
Notice No. 1 of 2024 — established the eTA regime. A later amendment (Legal
Notice No. 93 of 2025) added a short exemption list on top of it. The
practical result, checked against the eTA portal directly:

- **Most nationalities now need an eTA before travel**, applied for online in
  advance — not a stamp you get at the airport.
- **A small exempt list** (Malaysia and Singapore, among others) gets 90-day
  visa-free entry instead, under the 2025 amendment's schedule.
- There's a standard single-entry eTA (around USD 30) and a five-year
  multi-entry option (around USD 185) for nationalities that qualify.

## The part almost nobody mentions: how long you can actually stay

This is the detail that gets flattened into a confident "90 days" on most
travel sites, and it's wrong. Kenya's eTA portal itself says your length of
stay is "determined at the point of entry" — it's the immigration officer's
call, not a fixed number the eTA grants you. The underlying regulation caps
a visitor's pass at six months at the officer's discretion, but there's no
guaranteed figure below that.

We don't publish the commonly-repeated "90 days" for Kenya, because we
couldn't find a primary source that actually says it. If you're planning
around a specific stay length, that's a conversation to have with your
airline or at the border, not an assumption to book around.

## What actually to do before you fly

Apply for the eTA in advance at Kenya's official portal — not a
third-party "visa service" site, several of which exist purely to charge a
markup for a form you can file yourself. Bring the approval, a passport
valid well beyond your trip, and be ready for the length-of-stay question to
be answered at the desk, not before you land.
