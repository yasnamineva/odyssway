Some things you buy without a second thought at home are restricted, or
banned, somewhere else. Nobody asks about them before a trip, because nobody
expects a problem. Here are a few we've checked against official sources.

**Short version:** leave nicotine pouches at home for Australia and
Singapore. Melatonin needs a prescription to bring into New Zealand, and in
the UK it's a prescription-only medicine.

## Nicotine pouches (Zyn, Velo and similar)

**Australia: not allowed, since 24 July 2026.** The Australian Border Force
says the traveller's exemption no longer covers nicotine pouches. A
prescription doesn't help either: personal importation is closed too.
Pouches found at the border are seized. This is new. Tobacco still has a
small allowance (25 cigarettes or 25 g), and vapes have their own limited
exemption, so don't assume pouches follow the same rules.

**Singapore: not allowed.** Singapore's health regulator, HSA, lists nicotine
pouches among prohibited items, alongside heated tobacco and vapes.
Singapore's border agency, ICA, bans chewing tobacco and imitation tobacco
products for travellers. There's no personal allowance.

## Melatonin

In the US, melatonin is a supplement on the supermarket shelf. Elsewhere,
it's often a medicine.

**New Zealand: only with a prescription.** Medsafe says you still need a
prescription to import melatonin that isn't approved in New Zealand, which
includes supplements bought abroad. With a prescription, the normal rule
applies:

- up to **3 months' supply**,
- a copy of the prescription or a doctor's letter,
- the original containers,
- and **declare it** on your arrival card.

Since 2025, New Zealand pharmacies can sell some approved melatonin products
without a prescription, but that doesn't cover what you bring with you.

**United Kingdom: prescription-only.** The UK's medicines regulator, MHRA,
says melatonin is authorised only as a prescription medicine there. It isn't
legally sold as a food supplement, so you won't find it in UK shops. UK
travel guidance doesn't give a personal-import limit for prescription
medicines that aren't controlled drugs, so we can't tell you how much you can
bring. We say so on the page rather than guess.

## One more: cold medicine in Japan

Japan controls pseudoephedrine, the decongestant in Sudafed. Products with
more than 10% pseudoephedrine need permission from Japan's Narcotics Control
Department before you travel. The [Sudafed page for
Japan](/medications/sudafed/japan) walks through the application.
