# Residence permit holders: how 90/180 works for you

<Photo name="permit-street" hero />

<InShort>
  Days in the country that issued your **residence permit or D visa** don't
  count. Days in **other** Schengen countries count exactly like a tourist's.
  That's the trap.
</InShort>

<Compare

  no={[<>Days in the country that issued your permit or long-stay (D) visa. <InfoTip label="The exact wording">Schengen Borders Code, Art. 6(2): "Periods of stay authorised under a residence permit or a long-stay visa shall not be taken into account in the calculation of the duration of stay on the territory of the Member States."</InfoTip></>]}
/>

<Callout title="The trap, in one example">
  You live in Spain on a residence permit. Over six months you spend more than
  90 days travelling in other Schengen countries. That's an **overstay** — even
  though you legally live in the Schengen Area. Cross-border commuters,
  consultants and families split between two countries hit this without
  realising.
</Callout>

## Count it in the [calculator](/calculator)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your home-country stay">Enter the dates you were in the country that issued your permit.</Step>
  <Step title="Pick that country and tick the permit box">"Residence permit / long-stay (D) visa issued by this country" — those days are left out.</Step>
  <Step title="Add your other trips">Everything else counts against 90 in any 180.</Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

- **Plan renewals.** A gap between permits isn't covered — those days count.
- **Old trips still count.** A new permit doesn't erase short-stay days used before it.
- **Keep your own records.** Borders inside Schengen aren't checked, so tickets
  and receipts prove your count.

<More summary="Does the EES count my days?">
  No. Holders of residence permits and long-stay visas are outside the scope of
  the EES (Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Art. 2(3)), so your crossings aren't
  recorded the way a tourist's are. Counting your days in other countries is
  your own responsibility.
</More>
