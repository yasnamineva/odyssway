# Residence permit holders: how 90/180 works for you

> **The short answer:** days spent in the country that issued your residence
> permit or long-stay (D) visa don't consume your 90/180 allowance — the
> Schengen Borders Code says such periods "shall not be taken into account".
> But days you spend in **other** Schengen countries count like anyone
> else's. That's the trap.

## The rule, from the regulation

Schengen Borders Code, Art. 6(2): "Periods of stay authorised under a
residence permit or a long-stay visa shall not be taken into account in the
calculation of the duration of stay on the territory of the Member States."

So if you live in Spain on a residence permit, your months in Spain are not
short-stay days. Your weekend in Lisbon, your week in Paris, your ski trip
to Austria — those are ordinary short-stay days, counted against 90 in any
180, exactly as for a tourist.

## The trap, concretely

A permit holder living in Spain who travels through other Schengen countries
for more than 90 days within a 180-day window is overstaying — even though
they legally *live* in the Schengen Area. Frequent cross-border commuters,
consultants and families split between two EU countries hit this without
realising it.

Our [calculator](/calculator) models this: add your home-country stay, pick
the country, and tick "residence permit / long-stay (D) visa issued by this
country" — those days are excluded, and your trips elsewhere are counted.

## What about the EES?

Holders of residence permits and long-stay visas are **outside the scope of
the EES** (Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Art. 2(3)) — your crossings aren't
recorded in it the way a tourist's are. Don't rely on the system to count
your other-country days for you: it's your own responsibility.

## Practical notes

- **Renewals and gaps:** the exclusion covers periods "authorised under" the
  permit. A gap between permits isn't covered — those days count as short
  stay. Plan renewals accordingly.
- **New permit, old trips:** getting a permit doesn't erase short-stay days
  you used before it — the window keeps rolling.
- **Keep evidence** of where you were: since border crossings inside
  Schengen aren't checked, your own records (tickets, receipts) are what
  proves your day count if questioned.
