# Dual citizens: which passport for Schengen and the EES?

> **The short answer:** if one of your nationalities is an EU country, the
> 90/180-day rule does **not** apply to you — it only limits "third-country
> nationals", and the law defines that as someone who is *not* a Union
> citizen. Travel on your EU passport and there's no day limit and no EES
> biometric record of your crossings.

## What the law actually says

The 90-days-in-any-180 limit comes from the Schengen Borders Code's entry
conditions **for third-country nationals** (Regulation (EU) 2016/399,
Art. 6). The same regulation defines a third-country national as "any person
who is not a Union citizen" (Art. 2). A dual citizen holding, say, both US
and Italian nationality **is** a Union citizen — so the short-stay limit
simply isn't about them.

The Entry/Exit System works the same way: its scope covers third-country
nationals admitted for a short stay (Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Art. 2).
Union citizens are not registered in it — no fingerprint enrolment, no
entry/exit records, no automated overstay checks.

## The practical part: documents

Border systems treat you according to the document you present. Two
consequences:

- **Entering and leaving the EU on your EU passport** means you're processed
  as a Union citizen: no 90/180 count, no EES record.
- **Mixing documents invites confusion.** If you enter on your non-EU
  passport, you'll be processed — and EES-registered — as a third-country
  national on that document. Use the same (EU) document consistently for EU
  border crossings.

Remember that airlines and *other* countries have their own rules: many
countries require their own citizens to enter *them* on the national
passport (the US does, for US citizens). Nothing here changes that — pick
per border, consistently.

## One nationality EU, spouse or family not?

Non-EU **family members of EU citizens** have their own regime: with a
residence card under the Free Movement Directive (2004/38/EC) they are
outside EES scope too (Reg. 2017/2226, Art. 2(3)(a)). Without one, they are
registered like other third-country nationals. That's a separate guide-worthy
topic — for now, know the distinction exists.

## If both your nationalities are non-EU

Then the 90/180 rule applies to you regardless of which passport you use —
the limit attaches to you as a person, and since full EES deployment your
crossings are matched biometrically, not per document. Count your days with
the [calculator](/calculator).
