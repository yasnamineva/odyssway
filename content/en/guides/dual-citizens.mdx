# Dual citizens: which passport for Schengen and the EES?

<InShort>
  If one of your nationalities is an **EU country**, the 90/180-day rule
  doesn't apply to you. Travel on your **EU passport**: no day limit, and no
  EES record of your crossings.
</InShort>

## Which situation are you in?

<FactGrid columns={3}>
  <Fact icon={<ShieldCheckIcon />} title="One nationality is EU">
    No 90/180 limit, no EES registration — as long as you cross on your EU
    passport.
  </Fact>
  <Fact icon={<GlobeIcon />} title="Both are non-EU">
    The rule applies whichever passport you use. Your crossings are matched
    biometrically, not per document.
  </Fact>
  <Fact icon={<PassportIcon />} title="Family member of an EU citizen">
    With an EU residence card you're outside the EES too. Without one, you're
    registered like other non-EU travellers. <InfoTip label="Legal basis">Free Movement Directive 2004/38/EC; EES Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Art. 2(3)(a).</InfoTip>
  </Fact>
</FactGrid>

## Which passport to show at the EU border

<Compare
  yesTitle="Your EU passport"
  noTitle="Your non-EU passport"

Use the same EU passport **consistently** for EU crossings — mixing documents
invites confusion.

<Callout title="Other borders have their own rules" tone="info">
  Airlines and other countries decide for themselves. Many require their own
  citizens to enter on the national passport — the US does for US citizens.
  Pick the right passport per border, and stick with it.
</Callout>

<More summary="What the law actually says">
  The 90-in-180 limit is an entry condition **for third-country nationals**
  (Schengen Borders Code, Regulation (EU) 2016/399, Art. 6). The same
  regulation defines a third-country national as "any person who is not a
  Union citizen" (Art. 2). Someone with both US and Italian nationality **is**
  a Union citizen, so the limit isn't about them.

  The EES covers third-country nationals admitted for a short stay
  (Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Art. 2). Union citizens aren't registered: no
  fingerprints, no entry/exit records, no automated overstay checks.
</More>
