# EES at the border: what to expect

<Photo name="ees-departures" hero />

<InShort>
  If you're a non-EU traveller on a short stay, no more passport stamps. Your **first** crossing registers your passport,
  **fingerprints and a face photo**. After that, each crossing checks you
  against that record and logs the **date and place**.
</InShort>

## Your first crossing

Your passport data, fingerprints and a face photo are registered at whichever
of the 29 countries you enter first. Expect it to take longer than a stamp.

<Photo name="ees-fingerprint" />

## Every crossing after that

You're checked against your record, and the date and place are logged. Larger
crossings increasingly use kiosks and e-gates.

## What the system does with your record

- It **counts your days** against the 90/180 rule, and flags overstays automatically.
- It **records refusals** of entry.
- It helps officers **spot fake or forged passports**.

## Before you go

<Steps>
  <Step title="Count your days">
    The system counts them digitally either way. Our [calculator](/calculator)
    uses the EU's official method.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep your travel records">
    Boarding passes and bookings are your proof if a crossing is recorded
    wrongly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Know how to fix a mistake">
    A record can contain errors, such as an exit that didn't register. You
    have the right to get it corrected. <InfoTip label="Legal basis">The EES Regulation, (EU) 2017/2226, gives you the right to access and correct your data (Article 52).</InfoTip>
  </Step>
</Steps>
