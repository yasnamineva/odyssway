# EES missing exit record: how to get it fixed

<InShort>
  With no exit on record, the EES treats you as an **overstayer** once your
  allowed stay runs out. Gather proof of when you left and ask any EES country
  to correct it — it must **reply within 45 days**.
</InShort>

## Why a missing exit matters

<FactGrid>
  <Fact icon={<AlertIcon />} title="You're listed as an overstayer">
    The system checks automatically for records with no exit after the stay
    ends, and border and immigration authorities can see that list.
  </Fact>
  <Fact icon={<CalendarClockIcon />} title="Your data is kept longer">
    5 years from the end of your allowed stay, instead of the usual 3.
  </Fact>
</FactGrid>

## First, check what the system shows

<LinkCards>
  <LinkCard href="https://travel-europe.europa.eu/en/ees/check-how-long-you-can-stay" icon={<DocumentSearchIcon />} title="The EU's official stay checker">
    Enter your passport and dates for an OK / NOT OK answer and your days left.
  </LinkCard>
  <LinkCard href="/calculator" icon={<CalendarClockIcon />} title="Our 90/180 calculator">
    Count your own days so you can say exactly which day is wrong.
  </LinkCard>
</LinkCards>

<More summary="Good to know about the EU checker">
  Border officers can also tell you your remaining stay. The EU's tool doesn't
  count time in the Schengen area that began before 10 April 2026.
</More>

## How to get it corrected

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collect proof you left">
    Boarding passes, e-tickets, bookings or payments that show where you were.
    An exit stamp in your passport is worth keeping too. <InfoTip label="Why a stamp">When the system can't record a crossing, the regulation's fall-back is a passport stamp (Article 21).</InfoTip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a country to write to">
    The EU lists one authority per country for **access and corrections**.
    Writing to a country you travelled to is the natural choice; for questions
    about your remaining stay, the EU points you to the country where you last
    entered or left.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send a written correction request">
    Who you are, which crossing is missing or wrong, the correct facts, and your
    proof — citing Article 52 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2226. Our
    [country guides](/ees) include a ready-made letter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get it confirmed in writing">
    If they correct the record, they must confirm it to you in writing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The deadlines

<DeadlineTrack
  points={[
    { day: 0, label: "You send your request" },
    { day: 7, label: "Passed to the country that made the record, if needed" },
    { day: 37, label: "That country has checked the data (at the latest)" },
    { day: 45, label: "Deadline for the reply to you" },
  ]}
/>

## If they refuse

<FactGrid columns={3}>
  <Fact icon={<LetterIcon />} title="A written reason">They must explain the refusal in writing.</Fact>
  <Fact icon={<GateIcon />} title="A complaint">To that country's data protection authority.</Fact>
  <Fact icon={<ScaleIcon />} title="A court">You have the right to an effective judicial remedy.</Fact>
</FactGrid>

Once an error is shown, the overstay must be removed from the list without
