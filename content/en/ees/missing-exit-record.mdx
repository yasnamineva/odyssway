# EES missing exit record: how to get it fixed

> **The short answer:** if the EES has no exit recorded for you, it will
> automatically treat you as an overstayer once your allowed stay runs out.
> Gather proof of when you actually left, then ask an EES **data controller**
> to correct the record — the EU lists one for every participating country.
> Under **Article 52 of the EES Regulation**, you can ask **any** of those
> countries, and they must **reply within 45 days**.

## Why a missing exit matters

Every entry and exit is logged with its date and place. The EES then checks,
automatically, which records have **no exit** once the authorised stay has
ended — and anyone identified that way is added to a list of overstayers that
border and immigration authorities can see. If the record is missing, the
system assumes you never left.

A missing exit also keeps your data longer: records without an exit are stored
for **5 years** from the end of your authorised stay, instead of the usual 3.

## First, check what the system shows

The EU runs an official
[“Check how long you can stay” tool](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/en/ees/check-how-long-you-can-stay).
Enter your passport details and planned dates and it gives an **OK / NOT OK**
answer plus your remaining days. Passport control officers can also tell you
your remaining stay. Note that the tool does not count time in the Schengen
area that began before 10 April 2026.

If the result doesn't match your own count, run your dates through our
[90/180 calculator](/calculator) so you can say exactly which day is wrong.

## How to get it corrected

1. **Collect proof of your exit.** Boarding passes, e-tickets, and booking or
   payment records showing where you were. If a border officer stamped your
   passport on the way out — the regulation's fall-back when the system can't
   record a crossing — that stamp is worth keeping too.
2. **Contact a data controller.** The EU's official
   [contact list](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/en/ees/contact-details-for-data-protection-matters)
   names, for each of the 29 countries, the authority that handles requests to
   **access or correct** your EES data. The EU suggests contacting one of the
   countries you travelled to; for questions about your remaining stay, its FAQ
   points you to the country where you **last entered or exited**.
3. **Send a written correction request** citing Article 52 of Regulation (EU)
   2017/2226: who you are, which crossing is missing or wrong, the correct
   facts, and your evidence. Our [country guides](/ees) include
   a ready-made template letter in English and the local language.
4. **Watch the deadlines.** The country must reply within **45 days**. If it
   has to consult the country that made the record, it must contact that
   country within **7 days**, and the check must be done within **30 days** of
   that contact. If they correct the record, they must confirm it to you in
   writing.

## If they refuse

A country that doesn't agree the record is wrong must explain why **in
writing**. You can then complain to that country's **data protection
supervisory authority** (also on the EU contact list), and you have the right
to an effective judicial remedy. Once an error is shown, the regulation
requires overstay data to be removed from the overstayer list without delay.
See [the EES says you overstayed](/ees/dispute-overstay) for the full set of
rights.
