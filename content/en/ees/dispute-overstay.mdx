# The EES says you overstayed — but you didn't

<Photo name="ees-letter" hero />

<InShort>
  You have a legal right to get wrong EES data corrected. Write to **any** EES
  country — not only the one that made the mistake — and it must **reply
  within 45 days**. <InfoTip label="Legal basis">Article 52 of the EES Regulation, (EU) 2017/2226.</InfoTip>
</InShort>

## How a false overstay happens

The system computes overstays from your entries and exits. So if **an exit
wasn't recorded** (equipment failure, or a crossing that didn't register) or
**was recorded wrongly** (wrong date or place), you can be flagged even though
you left on time.

## What to do

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather your proof">
    Boarding passes, tickets, accommodation and payment records that show when
    you really left. Run your dates through our [calculator](/calculator) so you
    can say exactly which day is wrong.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a correction request">
    Say who you are (as in your passport), which record is wrong (date and
    border crossing), what the correct facts are, and attach your proof. Cite
    Article 52 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2226.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it to one country">
    The country that made the error, or any other EES country if that's easier.
    Our [country guides](/ees) give the right contact and a ready-made letter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch the deadline">
    If the reply is late or a refusal, you can complain to that country's data
    protection authority or go to court.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<More summary="Your rights, exactly as the regulation states them">
  Article 52 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2226 gives you the rights of **access,
  rectification, completion and erasure** of your personal data, and of
  restriction of its processing:

  - Requests "may be addressed to the competent authority of **any Member State**".
  - The state "shall reply to such requests **within 45 days** of receipt".
  - If the state that recorded the data has to be consulted, it must be contacted
    **within 7 days** and must check the data **within 30 days** of that contact.
  - Data on the overstayer list "shall be erased without delay … and shall be
    rectified or completed" when you show you were **forced to exceed the stay
    by unforeseeable and serious events**, that you have **acquired a legal
    right to stay** — or **in case of errors**.
  - If the authorities refuse, you are entitled to "an **effective judicial
    remedy** to ensure the data are rectified, completed or erased".
</More>

## Before it gets that far

Keep your boarding passes: your own records make a false flag easy to
disprove. And count your own days — the [90/180 rule](/rules/90-180-rule)
applies whatever the system recorded.
