# The EU Entry/Exit System (EES)

<Photo name="ees-border" hero />

<InShort>
  No more passport stamps. Since **10 April 2026**, every non-EU traveller's
  entries and exits are recorded digitally in 29 European countries, and
  overstays are **flagged automatically**.
</InShort>

<More summary="What exactly is the EES?">
  An automated IT system that registers non-EU nationals travelling for a short
  stay, at the external borders of 29 European countries. It started step by
  step on **12 October 2025** and became fully operational on **10 April
  2026**. It is run by the EU agency eu-LISA under Regulation (EU) 2017/2226;
  the gradual start was set by Regulation (EU) 2025/1534.
</More>

## What gets recorded about you

- **Your passport details**: name and travel document data.
- **Fingerprints and a face photo**, taken on your first crossing.
- **Every entry and exit**: the date and the border crossing point.
- **Refusals of entry**, if you were ever turned away.

## Why it matters

Your days are now counted by the system itself, so a miscount shows up as an
overstay. <InfoTip label="How the counting works">The Schengen limit is 90 days in any 180-day period. Entry and exit days both count. See [the 90/180 rule](/rules/90-180-rule).</InfoTip>
Check your dates with our [90/180 calculator](/calculator), which uses the
EU's own method, or read [how the rule works](/rules/90-180-rule).
