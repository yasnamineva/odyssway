# The EU Entry/Exit System (EES)

> **The short answer:** since **10 April 2026** the EES is fully operational.
> It replaced passport stamping: every non-EU traveller's entries and exits
> across 29 European countries are now registered digitally, and overstays are
> **detected automatically**. First enrolment includes fingerprints and a
> facial image.

## What the EES is

The EES is an automated IT system for registering non-EU nationals travelling
for a short stay, operated at the external borders of 29 participating
European countries. It began operating progressively on **12 October 2025**
and became fully operational on **10 April 2026**, replacing manual passport
stamping. The system is managed by the EU agency eu-LISA and governed by
Regulation (EU) 2017/2226 (with the progressive rollout set by Regulation
(EU) 2025/1534).

## What gets recorded about you

- Your name and travel document data
- Biometric data: **fingerprints and a captured facial image**
- The **date and place** of each entry and each exit
- Any **refusals of entry**

## Why it matters for the 90/180 rule

Because entries and exits are recorded centrally, the system detects
overstayers automatically. Miscounting your days is no longer a paperwork
problem — it's flagged by the system itself. Check your dates before you
travel with our [90/180 calculator](/calculator), and read
[how the rule works](/rules/90-180-rule).
