# The Schengen 90/180-day rule, explained

<Photo name="rule-calendar" hero />

<InShort>
  You can spend at most **90 days** in the Schengen Area within **any 180-day
  period**. The window rolls every day — there's no reset date — and your entry
  and exit days both count.
</InShort>

## How the rolling window works

On **every day** of your stay, look back 180 days (that day included). Inside
that window you may have at most 90 days of presence. Days only stop counting
when they're older than 180 days. <InfoTip label="A useful shortcut">From the European Commission's guidance: after 90 days of uninterrupted absence, you can always stay up to 90 days again.</InfoTip>

Drag the slider to move the day you're checking. The bracket is the 180-day
look-back; the bar counts the days inside it.

<More summary="A worked example from the EU's own manual">
  A visa-exempt traveller visits for one day on 1 January 2024, then enters
  again on 1 June 2024 for a long stay. On 14 June the window holds 15 days —
  fine. Staying on continuously, they reach 90 days in the window on
  **29 August 2024**, their last allowed day. Staying to 20 September as
  planned would mean overstaying from 30 August. Our engine reproduces this
  and every other worked example in the manual in its automated tests.
</More>
