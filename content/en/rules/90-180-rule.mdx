# The Schengen 90/180-day rule, explained

> **The short answer:** you can spend at most **90 days** in the Schengen Area
> within **any 180-day period**. The window is rolling — there is no reset date.
> Your entry day and your exit day both count as full days. Use our
> [calculator](/calculator) to check your own dates.

## How the rolling window works

On **every single day** of your stay, look back 180 days (that day included).
Inside that window, your days of presence in the Schengen Area must not exceed 90.

This is why the rule confuses people: it's not "90 days per calendar half-year"
and it doesn't reset when a new year starts. Days only stop counting against you
when they become older than 180 days. One useful consequence, straight from the
European Commission's guidance: after **90 days of uninterrupted absence**, you
can always stay for up to 90 days again.

## What counts as a day

- The **day you enter** and the **day you exit** each count as a full day —
  even if you land at 23:50.
- Days in **any Schengen country** count toward the same shared limit: hopping
  from Spain to France doesn't restart anything.
- **Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland** are not in the EU, but they
  are in Schengen — days there count.
- **Ireland and Cyprus** are in the EU but not in the border-free Schengen area
  — days there do **not** count. Days in the **UK** don't count either.
- Days in Bulgaria, Romania or Croatia **before** those countries joined
  Schengen don't count. Our calculator handles this automatically when you pick
  the country ([full country data and sources](/sources)).

## A worked example (from the EU's own manual)

A visa-exempt traveller visits for one day on 1 January 2024, then enters again
on 1 June 2024 for a long stay. On 14 June, the 180-day window contains
15 days of presence — fine. But staying continuously, they reach 90 days in the
window on **29 August 2024**, which is their last allowed day. Staying to
20 September, as planned, would mean overstaying from 30 August.

Our engine reproduces this and every other worked example from the official
manual in its automated test suite.

## Days that don't consume the allowance

Stays under a **residence permit** or a national **long-stay (D) visa** are not
counted toward the 90/180 limit in the state that issued them. Days a permit
holder spends in *other* Schengen countries do count — a common and expensive
misunderstanding, covered in our [residence permit holders guide](/guides/residence-permit-holders).
And if one of your nationalities is an EU country, the rule doesn't apply to
you at all — see [dual citizens](/guides/dual-citizens).

## What happens if you overstay

Since April 2026 the EU's Entry/Exit System records entries and exits digitally
and **detects overstays automatically** — see [what to expect at the border](/ees/what-to-expect).
Consequences vary by country and circumstances; we don't provide legal advice.
