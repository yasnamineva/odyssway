# What actually happens if you overstay

<InShort>
  Overstaying does **not** trigger an automatic entry ban. You get a **return
  decision** with **7 to 30 days to leave**. Leave in that window and the ban
  isn't automatic. <InfoTip label="Where this comes from">The EU's Return Directive, 2008/115/EC — the rules every Schengen country applies. Quotes below are from its official text.</InfoTip>
</InShort>

## The process, step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="A return decision">
    Once you're found to be staying without meeting the entry conditions, the
    country must issue a return decision. It doesn't mean you're banned.
    <More subtle summary="What the directive says">
      "Member States **shall** issue a return decision to any third-country
      national staying illegally on their territory." — Return Directive, Art. 6(1)
    </More>
  </Step>
  <Step title="7 to 30 days to leave on your own">
    The decision has to give you time to leave voluntarily. It can be
    extended, for example for school-age children or family ties. Leaving in
    this window is how you avoid the next step.
    <More subtle summary="What the directive says">
      "A return decision shall provide for an appropriate period for voluntary
      departure of between seven and thirty days." — Return Directive, Art. 7(1).
      Extensions: Art. 7(2).
    </More>
  </Step>
  <Step title="An entry ban — only in some cases">
    A ban comes with the decision if you weren't given a window (for example a
    flight risk) or didn't leave in time. Otherwise it's optional. It normally
    lasts **up to 5 years**, longer only for a serious security threat.
    <More subtle summary="What the directive says">
      "Return decisions shall be accompanied by an entry ban: (a) if no period
      for voluntary departure has been granted, or (b) if the obligation to
      return has not been complied with. In other cases return decisions
      **may** be accompanied by an entry ban." — Art. 11(1)

      "The length of the entry ban … shall **not in principle exceed five
      years**. It may however exceed five years if the third-country national
      represents a serious threat to public policy, public security or
      national security." — Art. 11(2)

      Countries must also consider withdrawing or suspending a ban if you show
      you complied with an earlier return decision (Art. 11(3)).
    </More>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Fines and bans, country by country

The directive sets the EU-wide process. **Fines** are national law, so they
differ by country. Pick one:

<Callout title="Greece: overstaying is a crime">
  Unlike every other country here, Greece has criminalized simple illegal stay
  (Law 5275/2026): a first offense can mean at least **2 years' imprisonment**
  plus a fine from **EUR 5,000**.
</Callout>
